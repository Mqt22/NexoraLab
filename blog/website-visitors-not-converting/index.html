WEBSITE STRATEGY · CONVERSION

Your website gets visitors. So why aren't they contacting you?

A website can look polished, load successfully and still fail at its most important job: helping the right visitor take the next step. If people arrive but do not enquire, the problem may be less about traffic and more about the experience they meet.

Imagine someone finds your business through Google, opens your website and leaves a few seconds later. They may have been interested. But perhaps they could not understand what you offer, find a trustworthy reason to choose you or work out how to contact you.

More traffic is not always the first fix. Before spending more time or money attracting visitors, review the journey your website asks them to take.

1. Your message is not immediately clear

Visitors should be able to understand what your business does, who it helps and what makes the offer relevant without decoding vague slogans. A headline such as “Innovative solutions for tomorrow” sounds broad but tells a potential customer very little.

Use plain language that describes the actual service and outcome. Support it with a short explanation and one clear next step.

2. The next step is difficult to find

If a visitor has to search through several sections to find a contact method, many will simply leave. Place a clear primary action near the top of the page and repeat it naturally after important information.

Keep the action specific. “Request a quote” or “Discuss your project” tells people what will happen next more clearly than a generic “Click here”.

3. The page creates doubt instead of trust

People need enough information to feel comfortable contacting a business. Explain your process, show relevant work where available, provide accurate contact details and answer common questions. Do not invent testimonials, client logos or results; credibility is built through evidence, not decoration.

4. The mobile experience feels like an afterthought

A layout that works on a desktop monitor can become frustrating on a phone. Text may be too small, buttons too close together, navigation may cover the page, or important content may be pushed far down.

Test the actual mobile experience: read the page, open the menu, tap the contact action and complete the form using a phone-sized screen.

5. The page is slow or visually overwhelming

Large images, unnecessary scripts and crowded layouts can make a website feel harder to use. Compress images, remove features that do not serve the page's purpose and give each section enough breathing room.

Speed is not only a technical score. It affects whether visitors can reach the information they came for without friction.

6. Your content does not answer the visitor's questions

Potential customers often want to know what is included, who the service is for, how the process works and what happens after they make contact. If the page only lists service names, visitors are left to guess.

Explain deliverables and process in practical terms. Address the questions that commonly come up before a buying decision.

7. You are not measuring what happens

Without measurement, it is difficult to tell whether a change helped. Track meaningful actions such as form submissions, phone or WhatsApp clicks and visits to key service pages. Compare results over time rather than relying only on page views.

A simple website conversion review

Open your website as if you were a first-time visitor. Give yourself one minute to answer these questions:

  • Can I explain what this business offers after reading the first screen?
  • Is it obvious who the service is intended for?
  • Can I find a contact option without hunting for it?
  • Does the site work comfortably on my phone?
  • Do I have enough accurate information to take the next step?

Ask someone unfamiliar with the business to try the same exercise. Their questions can reveal confusing parts that you may no longer notice because you know the website so well.

The key idea:

A useful business website does more than exist online. It makes the offer understandable, builds confidence and gives interested visitors a straightforward next step.
