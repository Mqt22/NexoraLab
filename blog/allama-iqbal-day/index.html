Allama Iqbal Day: Remembering the Poet of the East

Every 9 November, we remember the birth of a thinker whose words asked people to look beyond their circumstances, strengthen their character and take responsibility for the future they wished to build.

On 9 November, Pakistan observes Allama Iqbal Day to mark the birth anniversary of Dr. Allama Muhammad Iqbal, born in Sialkot on 9 November 1877. Known as the Poet of the East, Iqbal was a poet, philosopher and thinker whose work in Urdu and Persian continues to be read, discussed and studied.

His legacy is not limited to memorable verses. Iqbal explored questions of identity, faith, knowledge, dignity and the role individuals play in shaping society. His ideas helped awaken political consciousness among Muslims of the Subcontinent and remain an important part of Pakistan's intellectual history.

A voice shaped by thought and learning

Iqbal's journey connected the literary and intellectual worlds of the East and the West. He studied in Lahore and later continued his education in Europe, where he pursued philosophy and law. These experiences informed a body of work that engaged with questions about the individual, society, history and the future of Muslim communities.

His poetry was not simply an invitation to admire beautiful language. It challenged readers to think, learn and examine the direction of their lives. That combination of reflection and action is one reason his work continues to resonate across generations.

Understanding the idea of Khudi

One of the central ideas associated with Iqbal is Khudi, often translated as selfhood. It is not a call to arrogance or selfishness. It points towards developing a strong, responsible sense of self through faith, knowledge, discipline, courage and purposeful effort.

In this view, a person should not remain passive or define their worth only through circumstances. Personal growth requires learning, making considered choices, accepting responsibility and using one's abilities constructively. Confidence matters most when it is joined with character and accountability.

Why Iqbal placed hope in young people

Iqbal repeatedly addressed the energy and potential of youth. His image of the Shaheen—the falcon—became a symbol of high aspiration, independence and the refusal to settle for a small vision of life.

The message is not that success arrives without difficulty. It is that young people should cultivate curiosity, strengthen their abilities and aim for meaningful work. Education should lead to understanding; ambition should be supported by preparation; and talent should be matched with service and responsibility.

Remembering Iqbal beyond a single day

National days of remembrance have value when they encourage more than a social media post or a ceremonial gathering. Reading Iqbal in context, exploring the ideas behind his poetry and discussing his place in history can make the occasion more meaningful.

We can honour his intellectual legacy by making time for learning, asking better questions, developing useful skills and contributing positively to our communities. These are practical ways to turn admiration into action.

A reflection for today's generation

Our world is changing quickly. Technology creates new opportunities, but it also asks for judgement, patience and a clear sense of responsibility. Iqbal's emphasis on self-development and purposeful action offers a useful reminder: progress is not only about what tools we possess, but also about the knowledge, values and character with which we use them.

The lasting reminder:

Do not let remembrance end with words. Let it encourage learning, thoughtful ambition, strong character and work that benefits others.

Remembering the Poet of the East

On this Iqbal Day, we pay tribute to Allama Muhammad Iqbal and the enduring place of his poetry and thought in our shared history. May his message inspire every generation to seek knowledge, build character and work towards a better future.
